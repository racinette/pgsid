Ordinal grammar mirrors DecodeDate/DecodeNumber. A digit run, then `-`, `/` or `.`, then a digit run not followed by the same separator is a two-field date. With a year of at least three digits and a three-digit second field in 1–366 it is an ordinal date; with a one/two-digit year it defers, because DateStyle decides (`00-001` fails in MDY/DMY, succeeds in YMD). Any other two-field date returns 22007, except an int4 field overflow at end of input returns 22008; with trailing text an overflow defers, because ParseDateTime lexical failures (22007) win. Whitespace-separated ordinals need a three- to five-digit year and a three-digit day followed by end, whitespace or `t`; six-plus-digit first fields stay compact dates. Three-field dates keep their earlier paths.

Day conversion reproduces date2j then j2date. date2j runs in i64 with every signed intermediate checked against int4; leaving int4 returns Unknown (PGlite wraps there, e.g. `2147483647-001` → 4453803-12-24; no portable signed-wrap contract is assumed). j2date emulates its two unsigned 32-bit additions modulo 2^32. That wrap is defined C behaviour and changes results: `4801-001 BC` natively becomes 4560-06-19 BC, while `4800-001 BC` and `5000-001 BC` fail. The private calendar conversion takes an ordinal day after compact_clock; constructors and non-ordinal parses pass 0. Conversion runs after clock-field and numeric-offset checks and before missing-timezone deferral, as ValidateDate does, so `0000-366 000000+16` is 22009 and `2000-367 000000+16` is lexical 22007.

English month dates follow DecodeNumber's textual-month rules, where swaps make results DateStyle-independent once one number has three or more digits. Supported shapes: month first (`Jan 1 2000`, `January 1, 2000`, `Jan-01-2000`), number-month-number (`1 Jan 2000`, `2000 Jan 1`, `01-Jan-2000`), optional leading weekday (`Mon, 03 Jan 2000`), whitespace/comma separators or one repeated `-`/`/`/`.` (DecodeDate fields, where any width is a plain number), followed by the usual clock/zone tail. Deferred: two short numbers (`Jan 1 00`), number-month-number with a first short number outside 1–31, short first numbers before a trailing month (`1 2000 Jan`, `00 1 Jan`), unknown words. The date parser's Julian branch now claims only `j`/`jd`/`julian`, so other words reach the shared grammar. Evidence: temporal-english-active/ (probe.log tabulates all three DateStyle orders). The dialect rejects `String::len()`; use index arithmetic.

Julian input follows DecodeDateTime's DTK_JULIAN path: j2date sets the date (the shared unsigned j2date emulation), BC/AD is ignored, and a fraction becomes the clock as `trunc(strtod(fraction) * 86400000000)` microseconds; the dialect has no f64→i64 cast, so the code truncates whole seconds and the exact remainder separately. A clock after a fraction is 22007; an integer day takes the ordinary clock/zone tail, and date input still applies clock/zone errors but only date ranges. After whitespace, `+`, `-` or `z` starts a zone for every date form (whitespace ends PostgreSQL's date field, so `-` is unambiguous there); `zulu`, `z1`, `- 05`, `-05:` and a clock after the zone defer. Deferred: `J1e2`, `J2451545.5e1`, `J2451545.`, `J2451545-05`. `Z` directly after any date now starts a zone (`2000-01-01Z`). Evidence: temporal-julian-combined-active/.

A fourth delimited field after a complete date is 22007 in every width, including four or six digits (natively not a clock on this path); an int4 overflow in it is 22008 natively and defers. Evidence: temporal-fourth-field-active/.

Meridians and weekdays: after the clock (adjacent or spaced) the parser consumes `am`/`pm` and weekday words before the zone; a weekday may also precede the clock, and `am`/`pm` alone after a date means midnight/noon. The hour check (above twelve → 22008) runs after zone decoding, so an invalid offset still reports 22009 first, and it is definite even when a timestamptz zone is missing. The same words may also follow the zone (`12:00+05 pm`, temporal-meridian-zone-active/). A spaced zone may also precede the clock (`2000-01-01 -05 12:00`): the parser records the zone span, parses the clock, then decodes the zone, returning 22009 for an invalid zone before clock errors (temporal-zone-clock-active/). Attached zones take the same path, and a leading weekday is skipped before compact, Julian and English dates but returns 22007 before any delimited date (temporal-attached-zone-clock-active/).
